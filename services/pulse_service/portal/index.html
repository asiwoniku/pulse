The office and the road
never disagree.

Every status change is decided in one database. What each side sees is decided by what that person is allowed to see — not by two copies of the truth.

Pulse

For the office

Partners, dispatchers and operations

  • Dispatch board. Move any order through the statuses the database reports as legal — and assign a driver from the people actually available.
  • Marketspace. Vendors send offers to carriers; an accepted offer becomes a real order in the carrier's own workspace.
  • Driver accounts. Issue a staff ID and password to each driver. No email round-trip, no shared logins, no mail server to configure.
  • Your brand. Your company name and mark sit beside Pulse's, so the workspace looks like it belongs to you.
  • Money and proof. Quotes, payment state and proof of delivery — including a one-time code from the recipient where you ask for one.
Pulser

For the road

Drivers and dispatchers in the field

  • Only your jobs. A driver sees the assignments handed to them, never the whole board and never the money.
  • Accept or decline. A job that does not suit is declined in one tap, with a reason the dispatcher sees.
  • Record what happened. Attempts, failures and delays become a real exception case instead of a silent gap.
  • Proof at the door. Recipient name, photo or signature, and the delivery code when the customer is expecting one.
  • Go on or off the board. One switch controls whether dispatchers can assign you work at all.

A driver cannot see price, payment or customer finances — that limit is enforced in the database, not hidden in the interface.
